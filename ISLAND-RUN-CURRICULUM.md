# Curriculum map and source notes

Reference check: 3 October 2026. This build targets ordinary English classes, not the separate multiple-foreign-language model.

The official 12 August 2025 English programme decision introduces the new ordinary primary/lower-secondary programme from grades 2 and 5 in 2025–26, progressively replacing the 2018 programme. For this prototype, the resulting grade progression is interpreted as Maarif for grades 5–6 and the regular programme for grades 7–8 in 2026–27. A school using a different programme can replace questions in the Teacher editor.

Official decision: [MEB Tebliğler Dergisi, September 2025, PDF page 26 / printed page 1350](https://dhgm.meb.gov.tr/meb_iys_dosyalar/2025_09/10163617_eylultebligler.pdf). The English rollout is subject-specific; the fact that newer grade 7 theme pages are published does not by itself mean that they are already in use by every grade 7 cohort.

## Grades 5 and 6: eight themes, ten game islands

The official Maarif model has eight themes for each of these grades. The game makes ten stages by splitting the neighbourhood/city theme and the universe/future theme. This does not represent two additional official units. Island subtitles are original game names; the selected-island panel shows the official theme family.

| Island | Grade 5 theme / stage | Grade 6 theme / stage |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | School Life | School Life |
| 2 | Classroom Life | Classroom Life |
| 3 | Personal Life | Personal Life |
| 4 | Family Life | Family Life |
| 5 | Life in the Neighbourhood & City — local places | Life in the Neighbourhood & City — local events |
| 6 | Life in the Neighbourhood & City — comparisons | Life in the Neighbourhood & City — transportation |
| 7 | Life in the World — food and restaurants | Life in the World & Culture — food and culture |
| 8 | Life in Nature — animals and habitats | Life in Nature & Global Problems — activities and solutions |
| 9 | Life in the Universe & Future — Planet Earth | Life in the Universe & Future — planets and weather |
| 10 | Life in the Universe & Future — holiday plans | Life in the Universe & Future — life in the future |

The grade 5 final theme includes landscapes and holiday plans; it is not treated as an advanced astronomy unit. Grade 6 adds the planet/weather/future content from its own theme. Some items recycle prerequisite vocabulary or language to keep the running challenges short and accessible.

## Educational design

The runner practises quick recognition of vocabulary, short sentence meaning, grammar in context and appropriate conversational replies. Translation is bidirectional. Gaps include singular/plural distinctions, tense forms, quantities, comparisons and functional language according to the theme. Questions vary between factual comprehension and choosing an appropriate response.

All prompts and distractors were authored for this game. They are practice items rather than copied textbook exercises. The model's broader learning outcomes also include speaking, listening and writing, which this runner does not assess. After a run, the answer review can support a short oral follow-up led by the teacher.

Soft and Hard change motor demands and available guards; they do not label learners or display ability rankings. Reading pace is a separate teacher choice. There is no displayed answer countdown and no score loss for an incorrect learning answer.

## Version 3 question expansion

v10.4.3 adds ten Turkish → English sentence and question translations per island, so there are 116 variations per island, 1,160 per grade and 4,640 across the game. Before v10.4.3 there were 106 original variations per island, 1,060 per grade and 4,240 across the game. Each bank contains 24 bilingual vocabulary pairs (48 translations), 20 English definitions, 22 sentence gaps and 16 applied questions. This release adds 1,120 variations to version 2. These practise a smaller set of learning targets; they are not 4,240 separate curriculum outcomes.

The additional contextual items include notices, timetables, short dialogues, comparisons, sequences, responsibilities and basic reading inference. Gaps vary ownership, singular/plural forms, verb forms, quantities, comparison, past events, plans, predictions and functional language according to the existing theme map. Some words and definitions are extension practice; use Teacher → Edit questions to match prerequisites, textbook language and class pacing. The runner does not claim complete coverage of every curriculum objective.

Definitions are classified as vocabulary, so a standard run always reserves two slots for applied questions, alongside two word and two gap challenges. Class/island history is shared across teams. Each type uses unseen IDs before recycling; a recent-family history also discourages repeating the same word through reverse translation or a definition, and repeating similar grammar patterns. Question-type order and answer lanes are randomized, with each lane correct twice in a six-gate run.

Teachers can edit every prompt, choice, key, instruction and explanation, or add and delete items. Each island supports up to 400 questions. Very small custom banks can repeat when alternatives run out. Edits and question history are saved locally and included in downloadable backups.
